The first concentrated build of 911 Simulator was aimed at getting beyond a static dispatch interface and into something that could track a player's session as a game.

That work added persistent game-state updates, including activity timing, and then built supporting systems around the experience: public-facing authentication, user management, orientation material, rewards, and the cleanup needed to present the prototype as a training demo rather than a collection of development screens.

The project also settled on **Silver Lake County** as its fictional setting and received the branding and demo-content pass that made the public-facing build coherent.

This was not the point where 911 Simulator became a finished game. It was the point where the underlying pieces were assembled well enough to put a playable demo in front of someone and evaluate the idea from the dispatcher's side of an emergency rather than from the field.

That remains the useful distinction for the project today: there is something real to try, while the larger simulator is still in active development.
